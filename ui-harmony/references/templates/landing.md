# Explain an offer or service entry

Layer: **templates** · Route: `landing`

**Use when:** Help visitors understand relevance and choose a next step.

**Avoid when:** Users already know the task and need a working application surface.

## Decision and composition

Distinguish persuasive marketing from utilitarian service entry. Explain who it is for, what happens and the useful next action. Do not automatically require a giant hero, gradient, logo strip and three feature cards.

Build a sequence from proposition to credible evidence, necessary detail, objections and action. Match proof to actual claims; fabricated customer logos, testimonials, metrics and scarcity are unacceptable. Keep repeated calls to action consistent in consequence.

## Behavior and adaptation

Test arrival with little context, long/localized copy and navigation to the promised flow. Decorative animation must not delay meaning or make controls elusive; respect reduced motion. Mobile order should preserve the argument, not merely stack desktop columns.

## Evidence and repair

Check that users can explain the offer and next step from available evidence. Measure heading wrapping, action visibility and layout stability; do not predict conversion from visual symmetry.

**Worked decision:** A public service entry needs eligibility, prerequisites and Start; a creative studio may need expressive work samples. The same hero template would serve neither automatically.

**Source boundary:** [Don Norman, signifiers](https://jnd.org/signifiers-not-affordances/) informs understandable action cues; page sequence here is original synthesis, not a universal conversion formula.
