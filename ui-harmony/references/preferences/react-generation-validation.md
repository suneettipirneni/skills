# React generation and bespoke transfer validation

Evaluated 15 November 2026. This is a bounded expert review of generated prototypes, not participant research, a blind comparison, a controlled old/new skill experiment or a universal aesthetic score. The user's latest criterion was original applications rather than reproductions of established surfaces.

## Protocol and provenance

Separate fresh-context agents received a frozen skill snapshot, task brief, output directory and port. They read relevant skill references, chose the architecture and visual direction, implemented React, inspected browser output and refined it. The root supplied no app CSS or desired pane arrangement. An independent adversarial agent challenged sources, guidance, actual screens and interactions. Root and adversary feedback went back to each generator; initial captures were retained.

The initial calibration cases were Gather (team directory) and Relay (subscription analytics). These established useful component/interaction baselines but did not satisfy the later bespoke requirement on their own. The second phase used skill-v2 plus the explicitly recorded bespoke supplement: **Margin**, a research-evidence workspace, and **Cue**, a podcast review workspace. Neither reproduces an Untitled UI screen. Their task/object relationships determine their composition.

The retained [protocol, source and evidence](../../../examples/ui-harmony/README.md) include the frozen skill manifest, original baseline/final screenshots, portable independent workflow checks, raw audit reports and generator evaluation records. Third-party reference captures are excluded. Cue's 53.318-second audio was authored for this test and rendered with system text-to-speech; its waveform and chapter timings derive from that file. Margin's interviews and participants are fictional. No private customer material was used.

## What was judged

The criteria were set before reviewing outputs: purposeful composition; readable type/content roles; coordinated component finish; narrow-screen recomposition; and a working consequential task with feedback and recovery. Bespoke acceptance additionally required the domain relationship to shape visible regions and transitions. Familiar controls were allowed; novelty and pixel similarity were not rewarded.

Both prototypes build. Each final independent suite scans four desktop/phone states with axe WCAG A/AA tags and reports zero violations. Incomplete checks are retained and manually adjudicated; they are not silently counted as passes. These checks cover Chromium at stated widths, not real-device touch, software keyboards, screen-reader sessions, Safari/Firefox or complete accessibility conformance.

## Failures that changed the result

**Margin:** the first phone layout hid which finding the Supports/Challenges actions targeted; a single initial finding concealed the ambiguity. The generator added the current claim and labeled count units, kept switching reachable through a long excerpt list, and corrected Edit to focus and reveal the intended title. A generic whole-workspace Undo could otherwise survive unrelated edits; it is now bounded at typing/save/create/switch transitions. Pale metadata was corrected while preserving the sage surface family.

**Cue:** the first timeline evenly spaced labels for 0,15,30,45 and53 seconds despite a 53.318-second duration. The 45-second label appeared near 40 seconds of waveform position. Ticks now use timestamp/duration. Transcript controls said Play while only seeking; labels now describe seeking. The first phone waveform clipped its flex bars; it now represents the complete recording. A dock observing the entire player stayed hidden when only 6px of the player remained visible even though transport had scrolled away. It now tracks transport visibility. Feedback content was corrected to match the actual synthetic audio. Secondary contrast and unsupported ARIA labeling were corrected.

**Earlier Relay calibration:** a whole-SVG scale reduced nominal 11–15px chart labels to roughly 6–7px on phone. Zero page overflow did not make that usable. The generator recomputed chart geometry at actual container width, reduced ticks and recomposed rows; exact data remained available. Root's40 final checks passed, including return focus/scroll after drilldown. Gather passed 24 root checks with invitation validation, confirmation, recovery and focus continuity. These are useful calibration observations, not extra bespoke successes.

## Tool applicability and numerical feedback

The bundled audit completed both bespoke applications at 1440×1000 and 390×844 with 0 capture errors. Margin produced 65 review occurrences (61 incomplete contrast,3 truncated linked-quote previews,1 visually hidden label); Cue produced 19 (12 incomplete contrast,5 visually hidden labels,2 small-target candidates). These counts represent detector output, not 65 or 19 proven defects and not aesthetic scores.

For Margin, manual comparison of actual text roles over its composed gradient backgrounds found the lowest sampled ratio at 4.580:1; timestamp/reference roles were 4.934:1 and quote/name roles 12.425:1. Linked previews intentionally truncate with access to complete source context. Offscreen scroller content was checked by bringing it into view. The hidden label supplies the editable finding's accessible name.

For Cue, the deferred active chapter timestamp was a real failure at4.224:1 and was corrected to4.968:1; the remaining sampled semantic roles pass. The search input's own short rectangle triggered target candidates, although spacing met the applicable exception. The apparent field now also focuses from its padding; geometry alone does not capture that behavior. Visually hidden labels remain intentional.

The audit's geometry cannot establish whether a linking action targets the intended finding, whether timeline ticks mean the right seconds, or whether the remaining visible strip of a player contains its controls. Those required task-specific probes and semantic review. This distinction is the central result: numerical feedback locates a discrepancy; the domain relationship establishes why it matters and how to repair it.

## Skill changes justified by the evaluation

The Untitled UI anchor now routes to task structure, workspace/workbench and specialist branches. Its operational trace is relationship → repeated operation → context that must remain visible → commitment/recovery → idiom and primitives. Workbench guidance now tests two objects, retained target identity, repeated phone switching and undo boundaries. Chart/adaptation guidance warns against scaling labels into microtext. Positive creation/refinement and headless React guidance remain separate from narrow repairs.

Style-fit weights remain unchanged. Craft is a narrative judgment with evidence and limits; a high preference total does not establish a finished product. Both bespoke examples use native controls where sufficient, so this run does not validate a Base UI implementation. The recommendation is an implementation option, not a guarantee of visual quality.

## Scope of the result

Root visual inspection and the independent adversary accepted both final outputs within the inspected states: coherent reading/working hierarchies, task-specific composition, coordinated controls, readable secondary roles and corrected mobile context transitions. This is an expert craft judgment, not an automated beauty result.

These examples provide evidence that fresh conversations using the skill, realistic domain content and its review loop can produce considered bespoke React interfaces. They do not prove that every first draft will meet the target or that the new guidance outperforms the previous version under a controlled comparison. Local storage, fictional data and limited fixed content make these prototypes reviewable; production concurrency, security, persistence and broader user research remain outside this demonstration.
