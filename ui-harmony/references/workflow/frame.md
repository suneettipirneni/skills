# Frame the problem and evidence

Layer: **workflow** · Route: `frame`

**Use when:** The task, audience, constraints or success criterion is unclear.

**Avoid when:** Repeating discovery for a scoped repair with known context.

## Decision and composition

Write the decision to be made before the requested artifact. A request for a dashboard may mean finding urgent work; a new card grid might worsen that task. Separate observed evidence, user statements, assumptions and open questions.

Create a short brief: actor and situation; job and consequence of failure; primary outcome; constraints and existing identity; available research/system; highest uncertainty; what is outside scope. For redesign, capture strengths before defects. For new work, model plausible tasks without inventing interview findings or personas presented as research.

## Behavior and adaptation

Choose an entry level based on uncertainty. A broken icon enters atoms; ambiguous information architecture enters structure. Ask only questions that materially alter decisions, while progressing on independent work. Update the brief when evidence contradicts it.

## Evidence and repair

Exit when there is enough context to choose the next experiment or repair. Record one observable success criterion and unresolved assumptions; business metrics need actual baselines, not estimated conversion lifts.

**Worked decision:** “Improve this dashboard” becomes “A dispatcher must identify overdue shipments and contact the owner.” Preserve compact table density; prototype priority ordering before changing shadows.

**Source boundary:** [GOV.UK discovery](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works) distinguishes the underlying problem from a proposed solution. The brief is this skill’s proportionate adaptation.
