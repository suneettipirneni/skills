## Shared acceptance criteria

1. **The main surface owns the consequential work.** Identify the user's next operation from the current task/state. Its supporting information is nearby or predictably reachable. Decorative metrics, project-card walls and summary chrome do not displace the work.
2. **Relationships are represented, not just named.** Selecting or editing one object changes the appropriate linked context. Source identity, time, target, revision or other essential scope is explicit. Renamed generic cards with disconnected actions do not qualify.
3. **State has domain meaning.** Distinguish selected object, current playback/reading position, authored draft, committed change, unresolved issue and completed review. Similar colors do not substitute for an explanation of these different states.
4. **Composition follows use frequency and comparison needs.** Persistent versus temporary regions are justified by repeated work. Separate-pane comparisons may be useful; collapsing into a focused phone view may be better. Neither simultaneous visibility nor fewer panels is a universal goal.
5. **Finish survives real content and state changes.** Check long excerpts/titles, multi-line notes, empty matches and error/recovery. Text roles, aligned metadata, control padding/material, focus and selection remain coherent. No novelty quota or reference-pixel similarity metric.
6. **A complete local loop works.** Find/select → inspect relevant context → make the intended change → understand outcome → recover or resume. Any simulated data/media behavior is declared; a still is not evidence of an audio/editor interaction.
7. **Narrow layout preserves the relationship.** A focused view may replace multiple panes, but users can return without losing selection/draft/playback context. Verify scroll and sticky regions with actual viewport space, not only lack of horizontal overflow.

## Margin: research evidence synthesis

Evaluate the supplied brief first; the following are probes of a source-to-finding relationship, not requirements to build a full research platform.

- Can a reviewer distinguish source material from an authored finding or interpretation without relying on color alone?
- Does a finding's evidence affordance reveal the relevant excerpt and provenance, not merely open an unrelated generic record?
- Can one evidence item support multiple findings, or a finding refer to multiple excerpts, if the actual task calls for that relationship? Do not force a one-to-one card model solely because it is easy to lay out.
- When adding/removing/reassigning evidence, is the affected finding clear and can the user recover? Preserve source content and unsaved finding text.
- If contradictory or weak evidence is represented, is its meaning explicit? A count of clips must not masquerade as research confidence. Do not invent a confidence percentage to make the design appear analytical.
- Does the reading area give meaningful excerpts enough line length and vertical space? Tiny quotation cards and excessive truncation can make a visually clean board unusable for synthesis.
- On phone, can the user move from finding to source and return with context, instead of scrolling through several unrelated full-screen panes?

Polish evidence: editorial reading rhythm coordinated with action controls; distinguish quotation, attribution, finding title and editable prose; understated relationship markers; a clear selected source/finding pairing. No requirement for a literal paper metaphor, notebook texture, graph visualization or unusual font.

## Cue: timecoded podcast review

Evaluate the supplied brief first; do not require production media infrastructure for an explicitly local demo.

- Does a note refer to a specific time and episode/revision, rather than a timestamp-shaped label unrelated to playback?
- Does selecting a note seek or otherwise reveal its intended media context? Does adding a note use a predictable current position, and can a user correct it?
- Is playback state distinct from note selection and resolution status? A selected row must not falsely imply playback has moved.
- When reviewing a note, are transport controls and relevant transcript/context reachable without losing the note draft? A large decorative waveform should not consume the working area.
- If a waveform/timeline is interactive, are its positions and scales meaningful? Provide appropriate keyboard and single-pointer controls; do not claim a static waveform is a working scrubber.
- Does resolution/undo preserve listening and selection context? A smooth animation that resets playback is a task regression.
- At phone width, does the transport consume a reasonable part of the working viewport and avoid hiding note actions? Do not shrink time labels and controls with a desktop timeline SVG.

Polish evidence: aligned timecodes, legible episode/note hierarchy, purposeful transport grouping, calm selected/playing/resolved states, and stable geometry during note creation. No requirement for a multitrack editing studio, album-art hero, fabricated audio amplitude or novelty control.

## Evidence and feedback format

For each material finding record: exact screenshot/state or interaction; observed relationship failure; consequence for the task; proposed local correction; strength to preserve; verification needed. Distinguish functional blocker, material craft gap and optional preference. Use computed sizes/hit bounds/contrast and observed focus/state/timestamp values to locate and verify a contract; no universal panel-width ratio, density score, maximum clicks or arbitrary latency tolerance.

A familiar composition can pass if its domain relationships are real and its finish is strong. A visually original application must fail if its central relationship is fake, inaccessible or loses context. Final agreement is scoped to the observed prototype and states, not proof that the skill reliably creates bespoke products on arbitrary briefs.
